MSE grows almost linearly with payload size; PSNR falls off logarithmically. Even at 50% capacity utilization, PSNR stays above 54 dB and SSIM above 0.995 — both conventionally considered visually indistinguishable from the original. This is why visual inspection alone cannot detect LSB steganography, and motivates the statistical/ML approach below.

Experiment C — Does class-weighting explain the false-positive bias?

scripts/experiment_class_balance.py

Three otherwise-identical Random Forests (n_estimators=300, same split), varying only how the 5:1 STEGO:CLEAN training imbalance is handled. Single held-out photo pair — see Experiment D for the 9-fold cross-validated follow-up.

class_weight='balanced' was not neutral — it was actively worse on nearly every metric. Removing it more than tripled clean-image accuracy and raised ROC-AUC, a threshold-independent improvement in ranking quality. This 2-photo finding was treated as a hypothesis, not a conclusion, and re-tested at scale in Experiment D below.

Random Forest (class_weight=None) is the production model, selected on TPR at a ≤20% false-positive budget — this app is a human-in-the-loop triage aid, so the operationally relevant question is how much real signal survives at a false-alarm rate an analyst could tolerate. Gradient Boosting wins on balanced accuracy and specificity instead, and would be the more defensible choice under a symmetric-cost assumption. See the full rationale on the Model page.
